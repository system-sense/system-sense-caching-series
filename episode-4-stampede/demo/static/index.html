Failure 1 · Cache Penetration

Requests for ids that were never issued. Redis has never heard of them and Postgres has nothing to return, so there is nothing to cache — and every request goes all the way to disk.

The fight · Cache Stampede

The dice · Probabilistic early refresh

The same key, left alone to expire on its own, over and over. A lock survives the expiry. XFetch arranges for there not to be one: every reader rolls β · δ · −ln(rand()) ≥ TTL remaining on its way out, and the one that wins rebuilds the key early, in the background, while the old value is still being served.

The bug · Releasing somebody else's lock

Hold the lock for 10 ms and let the rebuild take 25. The lock expires while its holder is still working, Redis hands it to the next reader, and then the first one finishes and deletes "the lock". It deletes the new holder's. A plain DEL cannot tell the difference; four lines of Lua can.

Release
Not run yet.

Lua does not make a too-short TTL correct — the lock still expires early either way. It stops that from turning into one worker deleting another's lock, which is the part that silently doubles your rebuilds. The TTL is the thing to fix.
